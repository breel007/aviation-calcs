What goes into the cost of flight training

The bill is almost always dominated by the airplane and the instructor. Dual hours are billed at the wet (fuel-included) aircraft rate plus the instructor’s hourly rate. Solo or time-building hours are the aircraft only. On top of that are ground instruction, an online or in-person ground school, the FAA knowledge test, the designated pilot examiner’s fee, a couple of hours of airplane time for the checkride itself, a medical exam, a headset, and books or an iPad app.

FAA minimums versus what people actually fly

Under Part 61, a private pilot applicant needs 40 hours, including 20 with an instructor and 10 solo. Under Part 141, an approved private course can be 35 hours, with a structured ground-school requirement. The Instrument rating (61.65) calls for 40 hours of instrument time including 15 with a CFII, plus 50 hours of cross-country PIC — some of which a private pilot may already have. The commercial certificate (61.129) requires 250 hours total time; a lot of the commercial bill can be time building. The CFI (61.183) has no Part 61 flight-hour minimum, but Part 141 Appendix F is a 25-hour course, and most programs also include two knowledge tests (Fundamentals of Instructing and Flight Instructor Airplane).

AOPA and many schools have long cited something like 60–75 hours for the average private pilot, not 40. The “national average” buttons on this page use planning figures in that spirit. They are starting points. Edit them.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to get a private pilot license?

At typical 2026 trainer rates, often the mid-to-high teens of thousands of dollars once you include more than the 40-hour minimum, the written, the checkride, a medical, and a headset. Your school’s rates will move that number.

What is the difference between Part 61 and Part 141 training costs?

Part 141 can use a 35-hour private course; Part 61 has a 40-hour minimum. In practice both groups often fly past the minimum, so consistency and how often you fly usually matter more than the part number.

How much does an instrument rating or commercial certificate cost?

An instrument rating is often 40–50 hours of instrument training plus tests. A commercial certificate includes the 250-hour total-time requirement, so time building can dominate. Use the buttons above and edit the rates.

What extra fees should I budget for besides the airplane and instructor?

Knowledge tests, the DPE fee, checkride airplane time, a medical, a headset, ground school, and a contingency of about 10%.
