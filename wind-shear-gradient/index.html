Wind Shear and Gradient Calculator

Wind usually changes with height. This page applies either the standard power-law profile — V = V_ref (h / h_ref)^α, with a default exponent of 1/7 — or a constant linear shear in knots per 100 feet, and reports the wind at a second height and the shear between the two. It is a smooth-profile estimate, not a microburst, an LLWAS alert, or a reason to ignore a PIREP.

Reference height must be greater than zero (a common surface-wind height is 30 or 33 ft). The 1/7 exponent is a neutral-stability rule of thumb over relatively smooth terrain; over water α is often nearer 0.11, and over rougher or more stable conditions it can be 0.25–0.40.

Power law and linear shear

The wind-profile power law is V(h) = Vref (h/href)α. With the common 1/7 exponent, a 20-knot wind at 30 feet becomes about 33 knots at 1,000 feet. The linear alternative, used when a pilot or a forecast quotes a shear in knots per 100 feet, is simply V = Vref + (dV/dh)(h − href). A negative shear (wind decreasing with height) is allowed; the page will not report a negative wind speed.

Neither profile is a microburst. Low-level wind shear in the AIM and in AC 00-54 can be a sudden, localized change associated with a thunderstorm, a front, or a land/sea breeze — not a smooth 1/7 curve. Use this page to turn a surface wind into a rough wind at pattern altitude, or to apply a quoted gradient, and treat convective shear as a weather-avoidance problem. Frequently asked questions

Why is the default exponent 1/7?

It is the usual neutral-stability rule of thumb over relatively smooth terrain. It is not a universal constant; type a different α if you have a better estimate for the site.

Can I use this for a microburst or an LLWS alert?

No. Those are abrupt, localized changes. This page only applies a smooth power-law or linear profile.

What reference height should I use for a METAR wind?

A standard anemometer height is 10 metres (about 33 feet). Thirty feet is a close aviation stand-in.
