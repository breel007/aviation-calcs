Parachute Descent Calculator

Once a canopy is open and flying at a steady descent rate, time to the ground is height divided by that rate, and drift is wind times that time. This page does that arithmetic, optionally computes terminal speed from mass, drag coefficient, and area (the same identity as the terminal-velocity calculator), and also reports the slightly longer time that starts from rest under quadratic drag. It is an educational descent-and-drift estimate, not a canopy flight manual.

Enter a descent rate directly, or leave it blank and fill in mass, Cd, and reference area. Height defaults to feet; descent rate defaults to feet per minute. Wind is an assumed constant horizontal wind.

Time is height over descent rate — plus a from-rest correction

If the parachute is already at terminal speed vt, the time to descend a height h is simply t = h/vt, and the horizontal drift in a steady wind is wind × t. A 1,000-foot drop at 1,000 ft/min in a 10-knot wind is therefore 60 seconds and one-sixth of a nautical mile. Terminal speed itself, when you do not type it, is vt = √(2m g / (ρ Cd A)), the same identity used on the terminal velocity page.

Starting from rest under quadratic drag, speed is vt tanh(g t/vt) and the distance fallen is (vt²/g) ln cosh(g t/vt), so the time is t = (vt/g) acosh(exp(g h/vt²)). From a typical opening height that time is already within a second of h/vt, because the canopy reaches terminal speed in a few vt/g time constants. A real ram-air canopy also glides, and a real opening is not a step to Cd A; use the canopy manual for a landing pattern. This page is the vertical-descent and drift estimate those manuals start from.

Frequently asked questions

How is this different from the terminal-velocity calculator?

That page finds v_t (and an Allen–Eggers re-entry peak). This one starts from a descent rate or a Cd A, then adds time-to-ground and wind drift, including the from-rest integration.

Does a ram-air canopy really fall straight down?

No. It glides. Treat the “descent rate” here as the vertical component and the “wind” as the net horizontal motion if you already know it; the page will not compute a glide polar.

Why is the from-rest time only a little longer?

The canopy reaches nearly v_t after a few seconds. From a thousand-foot opening height, almost all of the time is already spent at terminal speed.
