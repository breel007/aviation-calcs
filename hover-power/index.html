Hover Power Calculator

A hovering rotor is an actuator disk: disk loading is thrust over area, induced velocity is √(DL / (2ρ)), and ideal power is thrust times that speed. Divide by a figure of merit to get a shaft-power estimate, and, if you enter an available power, invert the same relation through the standard atmosphere for an induced-power hover ceiling. It is not a published hover-out-of-ground-effect chart.

Thrust in hover is the weight you want to lift. Diameter is the rotor disk, not a blade chord. Figure of merit defaults to 0.70. Altitude is used only for the density in the power at that height; the ceiling uses available power and ISA density.

Ideal hover power is T times √(DL / 2ρ)

Disk loading DL = T/A, induced velocity vi = √(DL / (2ρ)), and ideal power Pi = T vi. Shaft power is Pi / FM. A 20 kN thrust on a 20-metre disk (10 m radius) at sea level, with FM = 0.70, needs on the order of a hundred-plus kilowatts of shaft power — the exact figure is in the calculator. Given an available shaft power P, the minimum density that can still hover is ρmin = (T / (2A)) (T / (P FM))², which this page converts to a pressure altitude in the U.S. Standard Atmosphere, 1976.

That ceiling only knows about induced power and ISA density. It does not lapse the engine, add profile or tail-rotor power, or include ground effect, retreating-blade stall, or tip Mach. A HOGE chart in the flight manual always wins. Related tools: rotor tip speed, propeller static thrust, and drone flight time for a battery-powered multirotor.

Frequently asked questions

Is this a hover-out-of-ground-effect ceiling?

No. It is an induced-power ceiling at the figure of merit you type, in a standard atmosphere, with no engine lapse and no profile power. Use the flight-manual HOGE chart for a real number.

What figure of merit is typical?

Well-designed helicopter rotors often sit around 0.6–0.8 in hover. 0.70 is a round default.

Does ground effect change the answer?

Yes, in the real helicopter — in ground effect you need less power. This page is the out-of-ground-effect ideal disk.
