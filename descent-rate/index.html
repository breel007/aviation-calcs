Exact rate and the times-five rule

For a constant path angle γ:

ft per NM = tan(γ) × 6,076.12 ft/NM
descent rate (fpm) = groundspeed (kt) × (ft per NM) / 60

A 3° path is 318.4 ft per nautical mile, so the exact rate is about 5.31 × groundspeed. At 120 kt that is 637 fpm. The memory item “GS × 5” gives 600 fpm — a convenient, slightly shallow target that will not quite hold a 3° slope. This page shows both, and the same identity used on the top of descent calculator.

Other gradients

Some charts and training material use 300 ft/NM (the HAT/300 cousin of a 3° VDP) as a round number; that is a 2.83° path, shallower than a 3° ILS. You can enter a published angle, a chosen vertical speed, or a gradient in ft/NM and convert among them.

Frequently asked questions

What descent rate do I need for a 3° glideslope at 120 kt?

About 637 feet per minute. The GS × 5 rule gives 600 fpm, which is about 37 fpm shallow of a true 3° path. Use 5.3 times groundspeed if you want a closer mental check.

Is this the same math as top of descent?

Yes — the same tan(angle) geometry. The top-of-descent page starts from altitude to lose and tells you when to start down. This page is the glideslope / VSI form: given the groundspeed and angle you are flying now, what rate to set.

Should I use indicated airspeed or groundspeed?

Groundspeed. The path angle is a geometry over the ground. A headwind reduces the required fpm; a tailwind increases it. Use the groundspeed you actually have, including from GPS.
