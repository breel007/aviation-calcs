In-Flight Fuel and ETA Calculator

Once you are airborne, the useful question is no longer the preflight trip plan — it is whether the fuel now on board will get you to the destination with the reserve you still intend to keep. Enter remaining distance, current groundspeed, current fuel flow, and fuel on board, and this page returns ETE, burn, fuel at the destination, and a surplus or shortfall against the 14 CFR 91.151 / 91.167 planning times (or a reserve you type). It is an in-flight arithmetic check, not a substitute for a calibrated fuel gauge or a go-around plan.

Fuel flow and fuel on board use the fuel unit you pick. Distance and speed default to nautical miles and knots. The default reserve is the VFR-day 30 minutes of 91.151.

Remaining fuel is on board minus distance over groundspeed times flow

Estimated time en route is remaining distance divided by current groundspeed. Burn is that time times the current fuel flow, and fuel at the destination is fuel on board minus the burn. Endurance is on board divided by flow. Against that arrival fuel the page subtracts a reserve: 30 minutes for VFR day and 45 minutes for VFR night under 14 CFR 91.151, or, for IFR under 91.167, 45 minutes plus the time to an optional alternate at the same groundspeed. Those are the same regulatory times used on this site’s preflight fuel planner; this page is the in-flight remaining-distance check, not a second trip planner. An optional clock time (HH:MM) is added to the ETE to print an ETA. Every result carries its unit: fuel in the unit you choose, times as hours and minutes, the ETA as HH:MM, distance in NM, and groundspeed in knots.

What you should not read into the number

Fuel flow and groundspeed change. Gauges, unusable fuel, a headwind on the last miles, a go-around, and a taxi after landing are not in the arithmetic. Part 91 reserves are planning minima, not a target to arrive with, and this check does not replace a decision to divert while you still have options. For a raw time-speed-distance conversion without fuel, use the TSD calculator; for a point of no return, see the PNR page.

Frequently asked questions

How is this different from the fuel-planning calculator?

The preflight planner builds a trip with taxi, climb, cruise, and reserves from a planned route. This page starts from the fuel now on board, the distance still ahead, and the groundspeed and flow you are seeing, and asks whether the destination reserve is still intact.

Do I have to use gallons?

No. Pick US gallons, litres, pounds, or kilograms in the Fuel unit menu and enter fuel flow and fuel on board in that unit (gph, L/h, pph, or kg/h). Every fuel result is printed in the same unit.

Is a 30-minute VFR reserve enough to keep flying?

91.151 is a preflight planning minimum, not a target to land with. If this page shows you arriving below that reserve, treat it as a prompt to divert or change the plan, not as a legal interpretation.
