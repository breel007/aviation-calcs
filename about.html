About Aviator Calcs

Aviator Calcs is a set of 101 free, mobile-friendly aviation and aerospace calculators that run entirely in your browser. They cover the numbers most of us reach for before a flight and around the hangar: an online E6B for wind, fuel, true airspeed, and time-speed-distance; performance tools for density altitude, crosswind, weight and balance, glide distance, and climb gradient; weather helpers including METAR and TAF decoders, cloud base, and sunrise, sunset, and FAA night; cost estimators for aircraft ownership, loans, flight training, and rentals; an FAA pilot currency checker; and a set of standard atmosphere, aerodynamics, propulsion, and orbital tools. Browse all 101 tools on the home page.

They were built by an ATP, CFII, and flight test engineer who got tired of burying a phone calculator under an E6B and a POH. The goal is straightforward math, visible formulas, and enough explanation that you can check the answer — not a black box, and not a substitute for the book.

How the tools are checked

Each calculator is a static page: the formula is implemented in plain JavaScript, the math runs locally in your browser, and nothing you type is uploaded. Weight-and-balance loadouts, if you save them, stay in this browser. Before a change is published, that page’s test file is run against hand-checked reference values so a typo in a constant or a conversion does not slip through quietly. The page itself shows the formulas, the assumptions, a worked example, and the public sources the method is drawn from, so you can repeat the arithmetic by hand.

Keeping the site current

The catalog grows as new tools are added, and existing pages are revised when an explanation needs to be clearer, a test is tightened, or a public source document — FAA handbooks, the Aeronautical Information Manual, the federal aviation regulations, the U.S. Standard Atmosphere, or a standard engineering reference — should be cited more directly. We do not invent airplane-specific performance data. When a POH number is required, you enter it.

The calculators are planning and training aids only. Always defer to the Pilot’s Operating Handbook or Aircraft Flight Manual, official weather, and the official weight and balance report for the airplane you fly. The terms of use spell that out, and the privacy policy describes advertising and cookies.
