Finite-Wing Lift Slope Calculator

A wing of finite span has a shallower lift-curve slope than its section. This page applies the Prandtl lifting-line formula, with an optional Oswald efficiency, and Helmbold’s low-aspect-ratio expression, starting from a section slope that defaults to the thin-airfoil value 2π per radian. Both slopes are reported per radian and per degree.

Aspect ratio is b²/S. Oswald efficiency e defaults to 1 (an ideal elliptic wing) in the Prandtl formula. Helmbold’s classic expression does not use e.

Prandtl and Helmbold

Prandtl lifting-line theory gives a = a0 / (1 + a0 / (π e AR)). Helmbold’s formula, often the better first estimate at low aspect ratio, is a = a0 / (√(1 + (a0 / (π AR))²) + a0 / (π AR)). With a0 = 2π and AR = 6, e = 1, the Prandtl slope is exactly 3π/2 ≈ 4.712 per radian (about 0.0822 per degree), and Helmbold sits a little below that. At very large AR both formulas recover a0; at very small AR, Helmbold is the more conservative reduction.

These are incompressible, inviscid corrections. They are not a stall model and they are not a transonic lift slope. For the section slope itself see thin-airfoil lift; for AR from a span and area, see wing loading and aspect ratio; for induced drag from e and AR, see the drag polar.

Frequently asked questions

Which number should I use, Prandtl or Helmbold?

Prandtl with a realistic Oswald e is the usual lifting-line result at moderate and high AR. Helmbold is often preferred for low-AR wings (fighters, delta planforms). Compare both.

What if I only have a slope per degree?

Multiply by 180/π to get a0 per radian, or leave a0 blank and start from the thin-airfoil 2π.

Does this include compressibility?

No. It is an incompressible correction. At higher Mach numbers a Prandtl–Glauert factor, not implemented here, is applied first.
