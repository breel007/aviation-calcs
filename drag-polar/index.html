The quadratic polar

CD = CD0 + k CL²   with   k = 1 / (π e AR)

At (L/D)max, induced drag equals parasite drag:

CL* = √(CD0 / k)   CD* = 2 CD0   (L/D)max = 1 / (2 √(CD0 k))

For CD0 = 0.02, e = 0.8, and AR = 8, k is about 0.0497 and (L/D)max is about 15.85, at CL* ≈ 0.634.

Range and endurance lift coefficients

On this polar, the same CL* is the condition for propeller maximum range and for jet maximum endurance (maximum L/D). Propeller maximum endurance is at max CL3/2/CD, or CL = √(3 CD0/k) = √3 CL*. Jet maximum range is at max CL1/2/CD, or CL = CL* / √3. Feed L/D and speed into the Breguet range and endurance calculator for a distance or time.

Frequently asked questions

What is a typical Oswald efficiency e?

For a clean, moderate-aspect-ratio subsonic airplane, 0.7 to 0.85 is a common planning range. A well-designed sailplane can be higher; a dirty or highly swept configuration can be lower. The page will not accept a non-physical e.

Is (L/D)max the jet best-range condition?

Not on the usual Breguet jet-range argument. Jet maximum range is at max CL1/2/CD, a slightly lower lift coefficient than (L/D)max. Jet maximum endurance, and propeller maximum range, are at (L/D)max.

Can I use this as a flight-test polar?

No. A real polar includes camber, trim drag, Reynolds-number and Mach effects, and configuration changes. This is the textbook parabola used in conceptual design and performance class.
