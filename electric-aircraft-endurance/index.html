Electric Aircraft Endurance Calculator

A battery-electric airplane does not use a Breguet fuel-fraction cruise. Endurance is usable energy divided by electric cruise power, and range is speed times that time. Enter watt-hours and a power, or let the page estimate power from weight, speed, and a lift-to-drag ratio with a propulsive-chain efficiency. It is a constant-power energy budget, not a certified range.

Energy may be entered in watt-hours or kilowatt-hours. The default usable fraction is 90%. In the L/D mode, default chain efficiency is 80% (motor, controller, and propeller together).

Time is energy over power; range is speed times time

With usable energy E and a constant electric power P, endurance is E/P and still-air range is V × E/P. Twenty kilowatt-hours at 90% usable and a 40 kW cruise is 0.45 hours; at 100 knots that is 45 nautical miles. If you do not have a power number, level-flight power required is W V / (L/D), and electric power is that divided by a chain efficiency η (motor, controller, and propeller). Unlike the Breguet model, mass is treated as constant — the battery does not get lighter as you fly.

Climb, reserves, battery temperature, and a peukert-like rate effect are not in this budget. Use the airframe and battery manual for a dispatch range. For a hovering multirotor, the drone flight time page is the better model.

Frequently asked questions

How is this different from Breguet range?

Breguet integrates a changing fuel mass. A battery’s mass stays put, so endurance is just usable energy over power.

What should I use for η?

A combined motor, controller, and propeller efficiency. 0.80 is a round default, not a measurement of your airplane.

Does this include a reserve?

Only through the usable fraction. Lower it to leave energy in the pack at landing.
