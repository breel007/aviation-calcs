Thrust and power in level flight

q = ½ ρ V²
CL = W / (q S)
CD = CD0 + k CL²
Trequired = D = q S CD
Prequired = Trequired × V

If you enter a propeller efficiency η, shaft power is Prequired / η. Density ρ comes from the standard atmosphere at the density altitude you type (geopotential altitude in the 1976 U.S. Standard Atmosphere).

Speeds from the polar

Minimum thrust required is at (L/D)max, where CL* = √(CD0/k). Minimum power required is at CL = √(3 CD0/k), a slower speed — the same points identified on the drag polar calculator. Feed L/D and speed into the Breguet page for a range or endurance estimate.

Sources

Anderson, Aircraft Performance and Design and Introduction to Flight; FAA PHAK (thrust and power required, L/D); U.S. Standard Atmosphere 1976; the induced-drag factor k = 1/(π e AR) as used on this site’s drag-polar page.

Frequently asked questions

Is thrust required just weight divided by L/D?

Yes, in level unaccelerated flight: T = W / (L/D) = D. This page computes L/D from the polar at your speed and density, then T and P from that drag.

Why is the minimum-power speed slower than the (L/D)max speed?

Power is drag times speed. The speed that minimizes T (and maximizes L/D) is not the speed that minimizes T×V. For a quadratic polar, minimum power is at a higher CL, so a lower TAS — the propeller-endurance condition on the drag-polar page.

Should I enter indicated or true airspeed?

True airspeed. Dynamic pressure is ½ρV² with V the true speed through the air. If you only have IAS, convert it with the true-airspeed calculator first.
