Convergent-Divergent Nozzle Calculator

A convergent-divergent (de Laval) nozzle is designed so the throat is sonic and the exit is supersonic. Enter the exit-to-throat area ratio — or a design exit Mach — and a ratio of specific heats (default γ = 1.4). The page reports the isentropic exit Mach, pe/p0 and Te/T0, the ideal one-dimensional thrust coefficient, and, if you enter a back pressure, whether the nozzle is over-, perfectly, or under-expanded. Optional chamber pressure and throat area give dimensional thrust F = Cf p0 At.

Exit Mach from the area ratio

For isentropic flow of a perfect gas, the area ratio and Mach number are tied by the same NACA Report 1135 identity used on the isentropic-flow calculator:

Ae/At = (1/Me) [(2/(γ+1)) (1 + ((γ−1)/2) Me²)]^{(γ+1)/(2(γ−1))}

A C-D nozzle is designed on the supersonic root. For γ = 1.4, a design Mach of 2 has Ae/At = 1.6875 exactly and pe/p0 ≈ 0.12780. Ae/At = 1 is a sonic exit. The subsonic root at the same area ratio is shown for contrast; it is the exit Mach of a purely converging duct with that area, not of a started C-D nozzle.

Ideal thrust coefficient and expansion

The ideal one-dimensional thrust coefficient (Sutton / NASA) is

Cf = √{(2γ²/(γ−1)) (2/(γ+1))^{(γ+1)/(γ−1)} [1 − (pe/p0)^{(γ−1)/γ}]} + ((pe − pa)/p0) (Ae/At)

The first term is the momentum contribution at the isentropic exit (and equals Cf when the nozzle is perfectly expanded). The second term is the pressure thrust. Then F = Cf p0 At. Vacuum Cf uses pa = 0. Compared with the back pressure: pe > pa is underexpanded, pe = pa is perfectly expanded, and pe < pa is overexpanded. A strongly overexpanded nozzle can have shocks or separation in the diverging section that this ideal model does not include.

Frequently asked questions

What exit Mach does Ae/At = 1.6875 give for air?

Mach 2, for γ = 1.4, on the supersonic branch. The isentropic exit pressure ratio is pe/p0 ≈ 0.12780.

What is the ideal thrust coefficient at Mach 2, γ = 1.4, perfectly expanded?

About 1.208. The pressure term vanishes when pe = pa, so Cf equals the momentum term of the Sutton formula.

Does this include shocks in an overexpanded nozzle?

No. The page will label the nozzle overexpanded when pa > pe, but the Mach number and Cf are still the isentropic, shock-free values. Separated or shock-containing flow needs a different model.
