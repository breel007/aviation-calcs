Delta-v Budget Calculator

A mission Δv budget is the sum of the velocity changes you have to provide: gravity and drag losses, steering, insertion or circularization, plane changes, mid-course, landing, and a reserve, among others. Enter named items in m/s, km/s, or ft/s. The page totals them, optionally converts the total to an ideal Tsiolkovsky mass ratio from a specific impulse, and can compute a pure impulsive plane-change Δv = 2 V sin(θ/2). It does not fly a trajectory — for a two-impulse coplanar transfer, use the Hohmann calculator.

Adding up a budget

Unlike a single Hohmann transfer or a single Tsiolkovsky burn, a mission Δv budget is a list. Typical lines are gravity loss and drag loss during ascent, steering or residuals, orbital insertion or circularization, a plane change, mid-course corrections, a landing or braking burn, and a reserve. This page totals whatever you name. A low-Earth-orbit plus 30° plane-change example is in the loaded template: the plane-change term alone at 7.67 km/s is about 3.97 km/s — often larger than the Hohmann legs — which is why combined burns are used in practice.

The total is a scalar sum of non-negative items. It is not a vector, and it does not know whether two burns could have been combined. For a two-impulse coplanar transfer, use the Hohmann transfer calculator and paste those Δv values in as lines.

Mass ratio and plane change

If you enter a specific impulse, the ideal (massless-losses) rocket equation converts the budget to a mass ratio, with the same g0 = 9.80665 m/s² used on the rocket-equation and specific-impulse pages:

m0/mf = exp( Δv / (Isp × g0) )

A 9.5 km/s budget at 300 s Isp is a mass ratio of about 25.3, or a propellant fraction of about 0.96 — before any structural mass. That is a planning bound, not a vehicle design.

A pure impulsive plane change at speed V through an angle θ costs

Δv = 2 V sin(θ/2)

Doing the plane change at a slower apogee, or combining it with a Hohmann burn, reduces that cost; those combined maneuvers are not modelled here.

Frequently asked questions

What is a 30° plane change at 7.67 km/s?

About 3.970 km/s. The impulsive formula is Δv = 2 V sin(θ/2) = 2 × 7.67 × sin(15°) km/s. That is a pure plane change at that speed, not a combined Hohmann-plus-plane-change burn.

What mass ratio does 9.5 km/s at 300 s Isp require?

About 25.3, in the ideal rocket equation with ve = Isp × 9.80665 m/s². The matching propellant fraction is 1 − 1/25.3 ≈ 0.96, before structure or residuals.

Can I paste a Hohmann Δv into this budget?

Yes. Compute the two impulses on the Hohmann transfer page, then enter them as named items here, along with losses, a plane change, and a reserve, to get a mission total and an ideal mass ratio.
