Jet Engine Thrust Calculator

A turbojet’s net thrust is the change in stream momentum, plus a pressure term if the nozzle is not perfectly expanded: F = ṁ_a ((1+f) u_e − u_0) + (p_e − p_0) A_e. Enter air mass flow, exit and flight speeds, and a fuel/air ratio (or a fuel flow), and this page returns thrust and thrust-specific fuel consumption. It is a control-volume identity, not a cycle analysis.

Fuel/air ratio f defaults to zero if you leave both f and fuel flow blank (a cold jet). A perfectly expanded nozzle has no pressure term — leave those boxes blank. Speeds default to metres per second.

Momentum plus (p_e − p_0) A_e

Across a control volume around the engine, net thrust is the outgoing minus incoming stream momentum, plus the unbalanced pressure force at a non-ideally expanded exit: F = ṁ_a ((1+f) u_e − u_0) + (p_e − p_0) A_e, with f = ṁ_f / ṁ_a. A static, perfectly expanded engine with 50 kg/s of air, 600 m/s of exit speed, and f = 0.02 therefore makes 30,600 N, and TSFC is ṁ_f / F. TSFC is reported in kg/(N·s) and in the aviation unit (lbm/h)/lbf.

This identity does not invent an exit speed from chamber conditions — for a nozzle thrust coefficient from chamber pressure and area ratio, see the C-D nozzle page. For an ideal cycle efficiency, see the Brayton cycle calculator. For Isp and a rocket mass flow, see specific impulse.

Frequently asked questions

What if I do not know the fuel/air ratio?

Enter a fuel mass flow instead, or leave both blank to compute a cold-jet (f = 0) thrust.

When is the pressure term zero?

When the nozzle is perfectly expanded (p_e = p_0) or when you omit the pressure and area inputs.

Is a negative TSFC meaningful?

Only if the computed “thrust” were negative (a net drag). Check that the exit speed is larger than the flight speed for a jet that is actually producing thrust.
