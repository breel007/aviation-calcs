Vmo/Mmo Crossover Altitude Calculator

Jet and turboprop manuals quote both a maximum operating speed Vmo (a calibrated airspeed) and a maximum operating Mach Mmo. There is one pressure altitude at which those two limits are the same flight condition — the crossover altitude. Below it you reach Vmo first; above it, Mmo. This page solves that altitude with the same subsonic pitot identities used on the true-airspeed calculator.

Vmo is a calibrated (or indicated, if you neglect position error) airspeed. Mmo must be subsonic; the pitot formula on this page is the subsonic one. A typical transport pair is 330–350 KCAS and Mach 0.75–0.85.

One pressure where Vmo and Mmo are the same point

The subsonic compressible pitot relation connects CAS, Mach, and ambient pressure, independent of temperature. From Vmo as a CAS you get an impact pressure qc; from Mmo you get the ratio qc/p; the ambient pressure that satisfies both is p = qc / ((1 + 0.2 Mmo²)3.5 − 1). Inverting the U.S. Standard Atmosphere 1976 pressure profile then gives the pressure altitude of crossover. For 350 KCAS and M 0.80 that altitude is about 23,230 feet. The published AFM/POH marks, not this educational solve, are the limits you fly.

Temperature changes TAS at that altitude but not the CAS–Mach–PA relationship, so the crossover is quoted as a pressure altitude (or a flight level). For the underlying pitot identities and a TAS at a chosen altitude, use the true airspeed calculator; to express the result as a flight level, see flight level and transition.

Sources

  • Subsonic pitot-static formula as used on this site’s true-airspeed page (γ = 1.4, a0 = 340.294 m/s, p0 = 101,325 Pa).
  • U.S. Standard Atmosphere, 1976, pressure–altitude inversion.
  • AFM / FAA concept of Vmo and Mmo (the published mark always wins).

Frequently asked questions

Does temperature change the crossover altitude?

Not in this model. CAS, Mach, and pressure altitude are linked by impact pressure, which does not need OAT. TAS at the crossover does.

What if the page returns nothing for my pair?

If Vmo already corresponds to a Mach greater than Mmo at sea level — or the solved pressure would be off the standard-atmosphere scale — there is no crossover in the atmosphere this page models.

Is this the altitude I should change from IAS to Mach on climb?

It is the altitude at which the two published limits coincide. Your SOP or AFM may specify a changeover slightly different from that exact figure.
